NT51920, NT51930 and NT51931 stay retired under this amendment.

## Context

The legacy runtime contains partial or complete routes for NT51920, NT51925,
NT51930, and NT51931. Earlier `0.10.x` planning proposed migrating some of their
DPCMI, TP Header, CtrlRAM, processor, and support-matrix facts into the new
IC-first architecture.

The owner has withdrawn those four ICs from the `0.10.x` product capability
set. Retaining dormant profiles or migrating isolated facts would leave
multiple misleading forms of support: an IC could appear selectable,
resolvable, executable, or published even though the product no longer admits
it. The target model therefore removes the capability coherently instead of
keeping a partial compatibility route.

This decision does not claim that historical bytes, offsets, or golden
observations were incorrect. It changes the target product boundary.

## Decision

For `0.10.x`, NT51920, NT51925, NT51930, and NT51931:

1. do not appear in user-facing UI or CLI IC selectors;
2. do not resolve through `CanonicalCapabilityCatalog` as authorable or
   executable routes;
3. do not retain production composition profiles, CtrlRAM/Postbuild processor
   registrations, integrity routes, or output-naming admission;
4. do not appear as published Support Matrix rows; and
5. are not migrated into perfect-family or `SharedFactRelationship`
   declarations.

Historical source tables, golden manifests, test fixtures, reports, and ADR
statements may remain only when clearly labeled as non-production provenance or
legacy characterization. They cannot be queried as a runtime fallback or
silently re-admit a capability.

Issue #221 owns the coherent removal. Tickets #177, #187, #194, and later
consumer/UI convergence work must exclude these four ICs rather than migrate
them and delete them later. Compatibility adapters may temporarily read their
legacy evidence only while #221 removes all production callers; every adapter
must have a deletion criterion.

The `0.9.17` compatibility hot-fix has a narrower boundary: hide the four ICs
from user-facing selectors, but do not backport the `0.10.x` profile, processor,
catalog, or architecture deletion.

Reintroduction requires a new owner-approved specification, authoritative
firmware evidence, explicit authoring/publication decisions, implementation
ticket, and R3 review. Equality with another IC, filename similarity, metadata,
or a historical golden cannot re-enable the route.

## Consequences

- The `0.10.x` canonical catalog and Support Matrix have a smaller intentional
  denominator than the legacy runtime inventory.
- Migration tickets become simpler because they do not model facts for
  capabilities scheduled for removal.
- Historical `0.9.x` verification may continue to describe the former routes,
  but target-state documents must identify that scope explicitly.
- This is an intentional compatibility break during the early `0.10.x`
  refactor; no deprecation adapter is required for production execution.

## Verification

- UI and CLI selector tests prove all four ICs are absent.
- Canonical resolution, authoring-policy, composition, and processor lookups
  fail closed for each retired IC with a stable unsupported-capability result.
- Trusted profile/bundle/package inventories contain no production entry for
  the retired ICs.
- Support Matrix enumeration contains no publication row for them.
- Architecture tests reject perfect-family or shared-fact membership referring
  to a retired member.
- Repository searches may find the IC ids only in explicitly historical,
  non-production evidence or the retirement contract.
- #177, #187, and #194 tests prove no retired capability was migrated.
- Final R3 review records the exact removed registrations and confirms that no
  remaining supported IC loses shared canonical facts by accidental deletion.
